- but what the hell is intelligence?

- not just something I/you don't know
- many different aspects
	- internal processes vs. external behavior
	- rationality vs. intrinsic human, often irrational parts
- many different scientific fields
	- psychology - neuroscience - philosophy - biology - sociology - mathematics
	- probably more I did not think of or even know
- between disciplines - many parts, processes, capabilities
	- reasoning - creativity - abstract thinking - learning - self-awareness - emotions
- point I am trying to make - there is no universally accepted definition of intelligence
- AGI definition often abstracts to "human equivalent" intelligence
- but for in depth questions, exact definition is required
- influences - How AGI? - When AGI? - How to benchmark? - What if AGI?

- one last benchmark - different direction - away from language - to physical skills
- class of benchmarks - called physical Turing - geared towards intelligent robots

- more close to original Turing - **The** physical Turing test

- key ideas here - physical presence needed for intelligence
	- not just idea of robot enthusiasts - present in neuroscience/biology/philosophy

- so when? hard to answer - what about how to achieve AGI?
- two dominant approaches
- advancing SOTA neural network based models - most prominent
- most popular alternative - biologically inspired hardware and learning

- start with neural networks - approach familiar to most of you
- mainly just "more" - more parameters - more data - more modalities
	- parameter count image - note log-scale - exponential growth
- some new learning approaches
	- RLHF - optimize for human preferred output - better alignment than text probability
	- forward gradients - weight updates w/o backward pass - higher computation efficiency
- why this approach? - works best at the moment - best performance - most novel results
- several problems
	- data limitation - close to trained on "entire internet" - new data slower than growing models
	- efficiency
		- power - brain ~20W - LLM building sized super computer
		- data - imagine how much data foundation models see - compare to human age 20
	- distance from biological blueprint
		- artificial "neuron" more mathematical framework - very low relation to neuron cells
		- NN architectures completely different from brain physiology

- last point motivates second approach - bioinspiration
- idea - make computers closer to brains
	- spiking pulses - not accumulative/amplitude pulses
- neurons closer to actual neurons - needs special hardware
	- chips with several neurons - more neurons - even more neurons
	- 1 m cores - 1000 neurons a core - 1 b neurons - about 1% of human brain
	- potentially whole brain in future
- several unresolved issues
	- processing power - potentially easiest to solve - just make bigger
	- scanning resolution - chips no enough - need building/plan architecture
		- replicate brain needs full scan of brain - down to neuron connection level
	- conceptual correctness - philosophical discussion
		- perfectly replicated functionality - same result as original?
