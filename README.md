# AGI - Benchmarks, Feasability, Implications
The concept of human-level or artificial general intelligence (AGI) has been a much-anticipated technological goal for many years. Throughout the history of artificial intelligence, experts have repeatedly and incorrectly predicted AGI’s short-term achievement. But despite many impactful advancements in the field of artificial intelligence, no clear path towards AGI is known decades after its inception. This essay demonstrates that the main problem in the development of AGI lies in its ambiguous definition. It relates the vagueness of the term AGI back to its basis in the concept of intelligence and points out how the resulting ambiguity allows for individual and subjective understandings of
AGI. Additionally, it highlights the impact of this ambiguity on benchmarking strategies, technical approaches for implementation, the prognosis of development timelines, and predictions of impacts related to AGI. As a consequence of AGI’s intrinsic ambiguity, no targeted development of AGI is possible without a better understanding of the underlying concept of intelligence as a whole. Nevertheless, discussions on this topic rarely address the clarification of subjective conceptions of AGI or acknowledge the lack of a universal AGI definition.
